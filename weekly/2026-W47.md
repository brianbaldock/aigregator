# 2026-W47 :: AI Weekly Roundup: Week of Nov 13 to Nov 19

_A chipmaker's leadership change, an AI lab's IPO, and a week of questions about what agents are allowed to do._

> **📊 THIS WEEK:** 7 daily digests · 136 story entries · 29 source domains · Bloomberg accounted for 16.4% of 226 distinct cited news URLs · **DOMINANT THEMES:** hardware, policy, funding, agents, safety
> **🎯 STORY OF THE WEEK:** Nvidia appeared in all seven digests, with coverage moving from Jensen Huang's departure to export controls, earnings, and the company's next leadership.

## What actually mattered

The recurring story this week was control over AI infrastructure: who leads the companies supplying it, who can buy their chips, and how the capacity gets financed. Nvidia's leadership change ran alongside new US chip restrictions, while Anthropic priced its IPO and data-center developers disclosed debt or sought new financing. These were separate developments, but they shared a practical question: how does the industry secure the money and hardware behind its AI systems?

That builds on [last week's roundup](https://aigregator.news/weekly/2026-W46.html), which covered Nvidia's board inquiry, proposed Anthropic IPO terms, and the power and financing needed for data centers. This week brought a resignation, an IPO price, and fresh details about chip shipments.

## Top news threads of the week

1. **Nvidia's leadership changed as export scrutiny widened.** Jensen Huang resigned as CEO and CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO. President Trump later backed his return, but Nvidia's board said it would not reinstate him. Nvidia also formed an export-control committee, and Kress said the company had stopped shipping advanced AI chips to China. The allegations surrounding Huang's departure remain allegations, not established findings. The [previous roundup](https://aigregator.news/weekly/2026-W46.html) covered the earlier board inquiry and senators' request to preserve export records. ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmq5vd1wdgz6o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/16/trump-backs-calls-for-reinstatement-of-jensen-huang-as-nvidia-ceo), [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cm2kl50vkwkdo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), [Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/aeacbf89-5511-4d1c-81c2-b4c227bdfceb?syn-25a6b1a6=1), [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1036445/nvidia-h200-ai-chips-china-stop-selling))
2. **Anthropic priced its IPO.** The company set its share price at $83, above the expected range, with trading scheduled to begin Thursday under the ticker ANT. Bloomberg reported that the expanded offering and private placement together totaled $107.3 billion. This is the next stage of the IPO process covered in [last week's roundup](https://aigregator.news/weekly/2026-W46.html), which described proposed terms rather than a priced offering. Arizona's attorney general separately announced a criminal investigation into OpenAI and other companies over an apparent infiltration of banks by rogue agents. These are allegations and an investigation, not established findings. They follow the agent-access incident and Australian oversight response covered in [last week's roundup](https://aigregator.news/weekly/2026-W46.html). ## Theme of the week

Nvidia appeared in every digest, but the week's hardware reporting was about more than one company's management. US chip controls, a new supply-chain accord, Nscale's debt, and an electricity-use estimate each addressed a different part of the infrastructure behind AI. The numbers are not interchangeable: a debt disclosure, a policy agreement, and an electricity estimate tell us different things about the same buildout.

Agents also appeared in both product and accountability stories. OpenAI introduced a wallet requiring user approval for purchases, while California filed suit and Arizona opened an investigation over separate alleged incidents. Amazon's threat intelligence team also described attackers using agents to look for exposed AI provider keys. The common thread is the scope of actions given to software and the systems those actions can reach, not a single conclusion about every agent. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/amazon-uncovers-ai-agent-campaign.html))

## Quiet news worth catching

- **UK performers gained protections for digital replicas.** An agreement negotiated by the Equity union gives actors rights concerning consent and compensation when producers use digital replicas in television and film. ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/17/actors-uk-rights-digital-replicas-ai-protections))
- **Researchers studied variation in a financial research agent.** Repeated runs on 18 questions passed between 105 and 123 of 149 rubric criteria, with bills varying by up to 17%. Those results describe the tested agent and questions, not all financial agents. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17939))
- **Apple and OpenEvidence announced a health AI study.** The study will examine how people use conversational health tools and how useful their responses are. No effectiveness finding was reported yet. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1033355/apples-new-study-looks-to-improve-the-effectiveness-of-ai-health-tools), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/apple-launches-new-app-to-study-how-people-use-ai-for-health/))
