# 2026-W48 :: AI Weekly Roundup: Week of Nov 20 to Nov 26

_A chipmaker's return, an unverified mathematics claim, and the practical limits on what AI agents can do._

> **📊 THIS WEEK:** 7 daily digests · 137 story entries · 25 source domains · Bloomberg accounted for 17.9% of 212 distinct URLs cited in story sections
> **🎯 STORY OF THE WEEK:** Nvidia appeared in all seven digests, with coverage spanning its leadership, export controls and investigations.

## What actually mattered

The recurring story this week was control over AI infrastructure: who runs the companies supplying it, where their chips can go, and how much the hardware costs. Nvidia's leadership reversal and export scrutiny ran alongside reports of higher server prices and another proposed data-center project, this one in space.

That continues a thread from [last week's roundup](https://aigregator.news/weekly/2026-W47.html), which covered Jensen Huang's departure, Anthropic's IPO pricing and data-center financing. This week brought Huang's reinstatement and a close below Anthropic's IPO price.

## Top news threads of the week

1. **Nvidia reinstated Huang as chip scrutiny continued.** Huang returned as CEO and, according to Bloomberg, apologized to staff and acknowledged violating the company's code of conduct. He is due to face congressional questioning in December. Separately, the US plans to blacklist Megaspeed over allegations that it routed AI chips to China, and the Financial Times reports Nvidia has halted shipments to Southeast Asia. The allegations and investigations are not findings of wrongdoing. [Last week](https://aigregator.news/weekly/2026-W47.html) covered Huang's resignation and the earlier export-control response. ## Theme of the week

Nvidia appeared in all seven digests, but the infrastructure story extended beyond its leadership. The Financial Times reported that server suppliers sought price increases of up to 45% after agreements were signed; Bloomberg reported that Amazon agreed to acquire much of Decart AI for $7.5 billion. The latter is a reported agreement, not a reported completed acquisition. Server prices, acquisition terms and chip controls are different pieces of the same hardware and compute picture. ([Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/dd2a6594-6146-4ff1-ac8e-54749c1fc99f?syn-25a6b1a6=1), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/amazon-is-said-to-pay-7-5-billion-for-ai-startup-decart-s-tech))

The other recurring question was what happens when AI systems reach beyond a chat window. Muse's shopping access, Gemini's computer use and OpenRouter's provider incidents concern different systems, but all turn attention to the services and data those systems touch. Product access, permissions and data handling are not the same problem, and none of these reports settles the others.

## Quiet news worth catching

- **A Dutch watchdog warned against buying AI smart glasses.** Tests it cited found glasses misread medicine instructions and, in one case, falsely claimed to have called an ambulance. ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckq6eyrzrzy5o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))
- **Australia's musicians rejected a proposed training carveout.** The music industry's trade group opposed a reported exemption for musicians from AI scraping that would leave other creative sectors uncovered. No enacted policy was described. ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/nov/20/australian-music-industry-rejects-proposal-to-give-musicians-exemption-ai-scraping))
- **OpenAI and Cisco introduced Shirudo.** The companies describe the open-source Linux runtime as a way to protect systems while teams deploy patches. This is a vendor announcement, not an independent security assessment. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo))
- **The llama.cpp team published an AMD benchmark dashboard.** It compares inference on Ryzen CPUs and Radeon GPUs; results should be read against the tested hardware and configurations. It follows the project's version 1.0 announcement in [last week's roundup](https://aigregator.news/weekly/2026-W47.html). ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/ggml-org/llamacpp-amd-bench))
